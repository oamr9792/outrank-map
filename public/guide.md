# Quickstart: Outrank Map in plain English

## The idea in one paragraph

Your client Googles their name and a bad article shows up. To push it down, you need other pages about them that rank **higher**. But which websites can actually beat that bad site? This tool finds out by looking at hundreds of **other people's** name searches where the bad site shows up, and counting which websites keep landing above it. A site that beats The Real Deal for 80 other people will probably beat it for your client too, if it has a page about them.

> It's an educated guess from similar cases, not a promise. Ranking for your client still depends on the actual page you publish.

## The 5-minute version

1. **New client** (left side): type the client's name, then press *Search the name*. You'll see what Google shows for them (about half a cent).
2. **Tick the bad results** in the list, then press *Save problem URLs*.
3. Press **Run studies**. For each bad website, the tool studies who beats it. If that site was studied before, it reuses the study for free.
4. Look at **3 · Domains that beat every host**. The top rows are your best bets. Click a row to see the actual winning pages.
5. Press **Plan report**. It lists suggestions under sections 3 and 4: press ☆ on the ones you'll use and they move into the plan. Press the history button in section 2 to see whether each bad result is fading. Then print it, or save it as a PDF.

Every ⓘ in the app explains a number in plain words, and what to use it for.

## The workflows

### A. Start from a client (the usual way)

**1 · Google results for the name.** This is what a stranger sees. Tick the results that hurt. Flags like "civil-litigation?" are a guess from the headline, so check each one. Missing one? Paste it into *Add a problem URL*.

**Hosts to study.** Each bad URL is studied by its website. If the bad page sits on a subdomain (events.example.com), you can change it to the main site (example.com).

**2 · Studies per host.** For each bad site, the tool needs a study: the person-name searches it ranks for, and who beats it on each one. *Keywords per host* is how many searches to test. More is more reliable, but costs more. *Re-run fresh* replaces an old study.

**3 · Domains that beat every host.** The key table.
- **Merged reach**: how often a site beats the *hardest* of your bad sites. A placement has to beat all of them to help, so this is the number to sort by.
- **★ In name results**: the site already has a page about your client. Improving that page is usually cheaper and faster than a new placement.
- **vs each host**: the shaded cell is the bad site this domain struggles with most.
- **Situation profile** (default) weights searches that look like your client's case: same kind of page, similar position, similar fame, same kind of trouble.

**Name's search volume band** (top of the page): how often people Google your client. Pick a band if you know it, or fetch the real number.

**Explain shortlist.** For starred sites, it checks *why* they win:
- **No external links**: the site wins with pages nobody links to, so a fresh page there can rank by itself.
- **Median page age**: how long winners took, so you know how long to wait.
- **Name in title / URL**: put the full name in both.

**Plan report.** One printable page or two, in order: what Google shows, whether each bad result is fading on its own, sites you already have to strengthen, then new placements to go after. You can fill it in from the report itself: *Fetch 12-month history for every host* in section 2, ☆ on the suggestions in sections 3 and 4, and *Explain the shortlist* at the end. Suggestions and buttons don't print.

### B. Study one website (no client yet)

Use **New study (single domain)** to research a news site before you have a client, e.g. to know your options against The Real Deal in advance.

- **Min / Max words**: 2 and 2 means "first last" searches. 2 and 4 adds "name + employer" searches.
- **Target pos ≤**: only searches where the site is in the top 20 (or whatever you set).
- **Max volume**: set it (e.g. 5,000) to leave out celebrities.

On the study page, set the **situation profile** to match your client's case, star domains, then use *Explain shortlist*, *Report* or *CSV*.

**Does the target fade on its own?** fetches 12 months of history. "Fading" means time is on your side; "strengthening" means start sooner.

### C. All studies

Every domain across every study you've run. **Targets beaten** shows how many different bad sites a domain has beaten. Over time this becomes your own ranked list of reliable placement sites. It's free and gets better with every study.

### D. Placements and calibration

After you publish something, log it under **4 · Placements and outcomes** on the client page: the URL and the publish date. Every few weeks press **Check now**: it Googles the name once and records where your placements and the bad results sit.

**Calibration** (bottom of *All studies*) puts what the tool predicted next to what actually happened, across all clients. After a few months it tells you how much to trust the scores.

### E. Aggregator triggers (ZoomInfo, RocketReach, MarketScreener…)

People-aggregator sites build profiles from other sources. Getting your client onto those sources tends to bring the aggregator profiles with it: neutral pages that fill more of the top 10.

Under **Aggregator triggers**, enter the aggregator (e.g. zoominfo.com) and press **Estimate and run**. It shows the cost first: about 5,100 Ahrefs units and ~$1.50 for 150 people a side. It compares people who have a profile there with comparable people who don't, and reports:
- **Lift**: how much likelier the profile is when someone is on a given site.
- **First**: how often that site appeared in Google before the profile did.
- **Cited**: how often the aggregator's own pages link to it.

The top 3–4 under **Recommended sources** are where to get your client listed first. It's strong evidence, not proof: log what you publish under Placements and check later.

## Reading the numbers: rules of thumb

- **Use weighted reach to rank, and reach to sanity-check.**
- **Small samples lie.** If *effective contests* is under ~10, or *seen with target* is under ~5, treat it as a hint.
- **Distinct people beats raw wins.** Winning for many different people is more reliable than winning a lot for one person.
- **Access tells you the job.** Self-publish: do it today. Profile / directory: claim or create a listing. News: needs a pitch. Court / public record: a competitor, not a placement.
- **Page type tells you what to make.** If a site wins with profiles, build a profile there, not an article.
- **Already in the name results beats a new placement** at the same score.

## What the tool will not do

- It doesn't predict exactly where a single page will rank for your client.
- It doesn't use AI to score anything: every number traces back to a Google result you can click.
- It doesn't run anything on its own: every paid step is a button you press.
